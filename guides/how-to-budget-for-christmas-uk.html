How to budget for Christmas in the UK without a January hangover

Updated 2026-09-30

Start with one total, not a list of gifts

Most Christmas overspending happens because people start with the gift list and add up the total afterwards, when it is too late to change it.

Do it the other way round: decide the whole amount you can spend on Christmas first, based on what you can save between now and December without using credit.

From the start of October there are roughly 12 weeks until Christmas, so a total of GBP 600 means about GBP 50 a week put aside from now.

Split the total into categories

Gifts are usually the biggest part, but food and drink, travel to see family, and nights out often add up to as much again.

A simple split is gifts, food and drink, cards and postage, decorations, travel, and going out, each with its own figure that adds up to your total.

If last year's bank statements are to hand, look at November and December to see what Christmas really cost, rather than what you remember it costing.

Give every person a number

Write down everyone you buy for, then give each person a figure before you start shopping, so the gift budget is shared out on purpose.

For wider family and friends, a Secret Santa or a spending limit agreed in advance often comes as a relief to everyone, not just you.

Keep a note of what you have bought and what it cost next to each name, because it is easy to forget an early purchase and buy a second gift.

The costs people forget

Wrapping paper, sellotape, gift bags, batteries, and stamps for cards and parcels are small on their own but add up across the season.

Postage for gifts to family elsewhere in the UK or abroad costs more the later you send, and last posting dates are published by Royal Mail and each courier every autumn, so check them early.

Travel can be the hidden big one: rail fares, fuel, and parking over the holidays, especially if you are booking late.

Hosting brings its own costs, such as extra food for Boxing Day, drinks for visitors, and a spare room made ready.

Where to keep the money

A separate savings account or a named pot in your banking app keeps Christmas money away from everyday spending.

Money in a UK bank or building society account is protected by the Financial Services Compensation Scheme up to a limit, but Christmas hamper and savings club schemes are often not covered in the same way, so check before paying into one.

If you do buy on a credit card, a single item costing over GBP 100 and up to GBP 30,000 gets Section 75 protection, which can help if something goes wrong, but it only helps if you can clear the balance.

Shop with the plan, then check it

Take the per person list with you, online or in the shops, and tick items off as you buy them so you can see what is left in each category.

When you buy online from a UK trader you normally have 14 days to cancel after the goods arrive, and many shops extend returns over Christmas, so keep receipts together in one place.

Once Christmas is over, note what you actually spent in each category, because that is the best starting figure for next year's savings plan.

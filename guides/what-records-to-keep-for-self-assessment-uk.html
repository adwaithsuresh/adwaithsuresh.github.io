What records do I need to keep for self assessment? A UK sole trader checklist

Updated 2026-10-02

Who this applies to

If you work for yourself as a sole trader, you need to keep records of your business income and expenses so you can fill in a self assessment tax return each year.

You generally need to register for self assessment if your self-employed income was more than GBP 1,000 in a tax year, which is the size of the trading allowance.

The UK tax year runs from 6 April to 5 April, so the 2025/26 return covers income from 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026.

If you started trading in that year and have not registered yet, the deadline to tell HMRC is 5 October 2026.

Income records to keep

Keep a record of every sale or payment you receive, with the date, who paid you, what it was for and the amount.

Back this up with the documents behind it: invoices you have sent, till rolls, platform payout statements and your business bank statements.

Note any income that is not from sales, such as a grant or a tip, because it may still count as business income.

If you use one bank account for both personal and business money, mark which payments in are business income, since HMRC can ask you to show the difference.

Expense records to keep

For each cost you claim, keep the receipt or invoice and log the date, the supplier, what it was and how much you paid.

Common allowable costs include stock and materials, office costs such as stationery and phone bills, business travel (not your normal commute), advertising, bank charges, and training that relates to your current business.

Where something is used partly for personal reasons, such as a phone or a car, only the business share can be claimed, so keep a note of how you worked out that share.

Keep a mileage log if you use your own vehicle for work, with the date, the journey, the reason and the miles.

Simplified expenses and the trading allowance

Instead of working out actual car costs, sole traders can use simplified expenses for vehicles: 45p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles in the year, then 25p a mile after that.

There is also a flat rate for working from home, based on how many hours a month you work there: GBP 10 for 25 to 50 hours, GBP 18 for 51 to 100 hours, and GBP 26 for 101 hours or more.

If your expenses are small, you can claim the GBP 1,000 trading allowance instead of your actual expenses, but you cannot claim both against the same income.

Either way you still need records of your income, and a mileage log or hours log to support any flat rate claim.

How long to keep your records

If you are self-employed, keep your records for at least 5 years after the 31 January online filing deadline for that tax year.

For the 2025/26 tax year that means keeping them until at least 31 January 2032.

Records can be kept on paper or digitally, and many people photograph receipts and store them in a folder for each tax year.

HMRC can charge a penalty if your records are not accurate, complete and readable, so it is worth checking that photos are clear before you throw the paper away.

Key dates for the 2025/26 return

Paper returns are due by 31 October 2026, and online returns by 31 January 2027.

Any tax you owe for 2025/26 is also due by 31 January 2027, along with your first payment on account for 2026/27 if one applies, and a second payment on account is due by 31 July 2027.

Payments on account usually apply when your last self assessment bill was more than GBP 1,000, unless most of your tax was already taken at source.

Missing the filing deadline brings an automatic GBP 100 penalty, even if you owe no tax, with more added the longer the return is late.

A simple routine that keeps January calm

The easiest way to avoid a January scramble is to update your records little and often, for example once a week or at the end of each month.

Log income and expenses in one place, set aside a share of each payment for tax in a separate pot, and keep a running total so the figures for your return are ready when you need them.

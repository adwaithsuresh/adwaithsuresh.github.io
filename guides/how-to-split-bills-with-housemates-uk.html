How to split bills with housemates in the UK: a fair method that avoids arguments

Updated 2026-09-30

Start with a list of every shared bill

Before anyone agrees a split, write down every bill the house pays, how often it is paid, and whose name is on the account.

A typical UK shared house has rent, council tax, gas and electricity, water, broadband, and sometimes a TV licence, contents insurance, a cleaner and shared shopping.

Convert everything to a monthly figure first, so a quarterly water bill or a yearly licence does not get forgotten or double counted.

Pick a split method for each bill, not one for the whole house

An equal split is simplest and works well for broadband, water and cleaning, where everyone gets roughly the same benefit.

Rent is often split by room size instead, so the person with the biggest room or an en suite pays more, and it helps to agree the percentages in writing before you move in.

Couples sharing a home sometimes split by income share rather than 50/50, so each person pays the same proportion of what they earn.

Optional extras, such as a streaming service one person does not use, should only be split between the people who opted in.

Council tax: who pays and what discounts apply

Council tax is charged per property, not per person, and on a joint tenancy the adults living there can usually be held jointly liable for the whole bill, so one person not paying affects everyone.

A home where everyone is a full-time student is normally exempt, but you need to send your council proof such as a student certificate from your university or college.

If only one adult counts for council tax, for example one worker living with full-time students, the bill usually gets a 25% discount, and it is fair for the house to agree who that saving belongs to.

Council tax is normally collected in 10 monthly instalments from April, and you can ask your council to spread it over 12, which makes the monthly split easier.

Energy, broadband and the TV licence

Take a meter reading on the day you move in and the day anyone moves out, and photograph it, because that is the only fair way to split energy used before and after a change.

Broadband contracts often run for 12 to 24 months, so agree up front who is named on the account and what happens if they leave before the contract ends.

If you share a house on one joint tenancy, one TV licence usually covers the household, but if each person has their own separate tenancy agreement, each person who watches live TV or uses BBC iPlayer in their room may need their own licence.

Moving in and out mid-month

When someone leaves partway through a month, split that month's bills by the number of days each person lived there rather than arguing over whole months.

For example, a GBP 90 energy bill for a 30 day month works out at GBP 3 a day, so someone who stayed 10 days pays GBP 30 of it, divided with whoever else was there over those days.

For energy, use the meter readings from the move date instead if usage was very different before and after.

Settle the leaver's share before they go, since chasing a former housemate for a small amount rarely works.

Settling up without a pile of small transfers

Bills are usually paid by different people, so each month add up what every housemate has paid to providers and what their share should have been.

The difference is their net balance, and each person then pays in or receives only that one amount, instead of everyone sending each other money for every bill.

Keep a simple record of who paid what and tick it off each month, because disputes usually come from memory rather than money.

Moving to the UK checklist: what to sort out in your first week, month and 90 days

Updated 2026-10-09

Before you travel

Most people now prove their UK immigration status with an eVisa, viewed through a UKVI account on GOV.UK, rather than a physical card, so make sure you can sign in to your account and that your passport details on it are up to date.

Carry your passport, any job offer or university letter, your driving licence, and copies of prescriptions or medical notes, as you will need them before you have a UK address to post things to.

Plan where your first letters will go, because banks, councils and employers often ask for proof of address, and a short let or a friend's address can cover the first few weeks.

Write every account number, reference and login in one place, as you will be asked for the same details again and again in the first month.

Your first week: work, bank and National Insurance

Your employer must check your right to work before you start, and if you have an eVisa you give them a share code from the prove your right to work service on GOV.UK.

If you do not already have a National Insurance number, apply for one online on GOV.UK, and you can usually start work before it arrives as long as you can prove your right to work.

Open a UK current account early, since your salary, rent and most bills will need one, and ask your bank which documents it accepts if you do not yet have a utility bill in your name.

Give your employer or university your new address, phone number and bank details as soon as you have them, so your first pay or loan does not go astray.

Register with a GP straight away

In England you do not need proof of address or immigration status to register with a GP surgery, and registering is free, so do it in your first week rather than waiting until you are ill.

Call 999 in an emergency, and 111 for urgent medical help that is not an emergency, which works across the UK.

Prescriptions have a set charge per item in England, but they are free in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

NHS dental places can be hard to find in some areas, so contact a few local practices early and ask whether they are taking new NHS patients.

Renting a home

In England, landlords or letting agents must check that you have the right to rent before a tenancy starts, so have your share code or documents ready when you apply.

In England, a tenancy deposit is capped at 5 weeks' rent where the annual rent is under GBP 50,000, and in England and Wales the landlord must protect it in a government approved scheme within 30 days and give you the details.

On move in day, take dated photos of every room, check the inventory line by line, and note the gas, electricity and water meter readings.

Ask who you pay for energy, water and broadband, as some rents include bills and many do not.

Council tax and other household bills

In England, Scotland and Wales, tell your local council you have moved in so it can set up your council tax account, which is usually paid in 10 monthly instalments, and Northern Ireland uses domestic rates instead.

If you are the only adult in the home you can claim a 25% single person discount, and homes where everyone is a full time student can be exempt, but you have to apply for both.

You need a TV licence to watch or record live TV on any channel or device, or to use the BBC's own on demand service, and you do not need one if you only stream other on demand services.

Set up energy and water in your name from your move in date, so you are not charged for the previous tenant's use.

Driving in the UK

If you hold a full licence from another country, you can usually drive in Great Britain on it for up to 12 months from when you become resident.

Licences from some countries, including EU countries, can be exchanged for a UK licence, while others mean taking the UK theory and practical tests, so check your country's rule on GOV.UK early, as test waiting times can be long.

If you buy a car, it needs tax, insurance and, once it is more than 3 years old, a yearly MOT test.

Note the date you became resident, because the 12 month window is counted from it.

Tax code, pension and your first 90 days

The UK tax year runs from 6 April to 5 April, and the standard Personal Allowance for 2026 to 2027 is GBP 12,570, so check your first payslip shows a tax code, as an emergency code can mean paying too much until it is corrected.

Set up a personal tax account on GOV.UK, where you can see your tax code, National Insurance record and employment details in one place.

If you are aged 22 or over, under State Pension age and earn more than GBP 10,000 a year, your employer must enrol you in a workplace pension, and you will get a letter about it.
